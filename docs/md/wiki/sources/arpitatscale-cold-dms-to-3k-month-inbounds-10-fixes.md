# Arpit（@arpitatscale）：从群发冷 DM 到每月 $3k 自然询盘——我改掉的 10 件事

> 作者宣布上月发出了人生最后一条冷 DM：从每天群发上百条，变成拿到 $3k 的自然询盘客户、对方在通话前就已信任他；他强调没换技能、服务或细分，只改了 profile 和内容，花约 90 天、10 个修正。第 1 件是把 job title 从 bio 里去掉——「social media strategist」是品类，「d2c 品牌 Instagram 有 10k 粉丝但零销量时会找的那个人」才是情境，并给出社媒运营、文案、设计、内容剪辑、投放五套带 {number}、{timeframe}、{X} 占位符的 bio 模板。第 2 件是置顶帖只回答一个问题「我凭什么把钱交给这个人」，有客户战绩时用 DM 截图、结果对比（他举例 open rate 从 12% 到 34% 比「我擅长邮件营销」更有说服力）、首周流程拆解、失误复盘、1-5 分钟自我介绍视频；没客户时用反向拆解同行、first-10-seconds 检查框架、日常错误、给自己或假品牌做一份、hot take。第 3 件是喂满 portfolio posts：最近 10 条里至少 7 条要让潜在客户产生「他处理的正是我的问题」之类的判断，其余 3 条留给个性。第 4 件是签新客当天先花 2 小时批量写 10-14 条铺满两周，标准不是日更而是「别几周彻底消失」。第 5 件是从同行视角切到客户视角，例如改写 headline 让转化率从 0.3% 到 1.2%。第 6 到 10 件分别是：用免费 lead magnet 替代冷外呼、出现在客户真实聚集处（关键词搜索、竞品粉丝、just raised funding / just launched 帖子、ICP 在听的播客评论区）、公开贴出不需要晒收入的微小胜利（他列了 18 条）、不要把报价公开挂出（$2k 会吓跑本可成交的人、吸引只会比价客户，而「$500/mo content strategy」会让已融资团队直接判定你不专业，他从固定 $500/mo 改为按客户价值定到 $2k）、以及按平台结构而非逐字复制来 repurpose（X 短而冲、LinkedIn 故事化、Instagram 视觉优先且静音可懂、YouTube 深度且标题缩略图占 80% 工作量）。

not proud of it but man enough to say it

### LAST MONTH I SENT MY LAST COLD DM, EVER

simply, because i don’t need them anymore

my journey went from spamming 100s of them a day to getting $3k inbound clients who already trust me before we ever get on a call

and interestingly i didn’t changed any of my skills, services or niche to get there

the only thing that changed was my profile and content

took me about 90 days and 10 specific fixes to get here

some were obvious in hindsight, most i had completely wrong for months

this article breaks down every single one:

→ why your bio is getting you ignored (and what to write instead)

→ the one question your pinned post needs to answer

→ how to turn your feed into a portfolio without posting “case studies”

→ the consistency hack that keeps you visible while you’re busy with client work

→ why your content attracts other freelancers instead of buyers

→ how a free lead magnet replaces cold outreach entirely

→ where your clients actually hang out online (you’re probably not there)

→ the wins you’re sitting on but never posting

→ why showing your rates publicly kills deals in both directions

→ the repurposing mistake that makes you sound like a broken record

### 1. removed my job title from bio

and here’s why it fixed my the sales funnel

- when someone sees your reply under a viral post or your content randomly pops in their feed, they think “who is this person”
- they tap your profile and the moment they see “social media strategist | helping brands grow | DM for collabs” their brain goes “ah, another one” and now they treat you like a “commodity” because there’s no differentiator between you and the 69 other people with the same bio (and this whole thing takes 4 seconds at max)

and the thing is, no matter how good your content/reply is, you could only manage to crack the attention not retain it

most people try to fix this by making the bio “catchier” or adding emojis or whatever

but that’s not the problem even, the bigger problem is you’re describing a category instead of a situation

“social media strategist” is a category

> “i’m the guy d2c brands call when their instagram has 10k followers but zero sales” is a situation

the second one makes people go “wait that’s literally me right now”

the trick is simple: write your bio like you’re explaining what you do to someone at a bar

you’d never say “i’m a growth strategist passionate about brand narratives”

you’d say “you know when a brand has a sick product but their instagram looks like a stock photo graveyard with 0 sales? i fix that”

that’s your bio (just a direction, please make it a lil more clean lol)

so tactically, this is how it looks like

bad: “social media manager | content creator | growth enthusiast”

worse somehow: “i help brands scale through storytelling and strategy”

what actually makes people follow you (feel free to steal):

- social media manager

“i turn dead {platform} accounts into sales channels for {niche} brands. last one (or add a client’s name for 10x credibility) went from {number} to {number} in {timeframe}”

- copywriter

“i write copy that sells for {niche} {clients} who hate ads. {last client}: {number} to {number} followers in {timeframe} with $0 ad spend”

- designer

“i design {deliverable} for {niche} brands. {X}% average conversion rate across my last {number} projects”

- video/content editor

“i edit content that retains attention for {niche} {clients}. portfolio: {link}”

- video/content editor

“i fix ad accounts for {niche} brands. {last client}: {X}x to {X}x ROAS under a {X$} budget”

### 2. made my pinned post just to answer one question

most freelancers either pin nothing (so the algorithm picks whatever performed best last week) or pin their highest like count post which is usually something generic about “lessons from freelancing”

honestly you can choose to do any of them but nothing will work in your favour

your pinned post is the one post someone reads after your bio convinces them to stay so it needs to answer just one simple question:

“why should i trust this person with my money”

and here’s how to draft it:

if you have client wins (even small ones):

1. DM screenshot: screenshot a actual DM where someone asked you a question about your skill and you gave a good answer (or make it up, the sauce is in your answer not who dm’ed you), post it as your pin. (people assume if you answer like this casually, you must really know your stuff)
2. “here’s what changed”: pick one client result and write it as: situation they were in → what you changed → what happened after. keep it specific but you don’t need to name them. tip: “their open rate went from 12% to 34%” is more convincing than “i’m great at email marketing”
3. process walkthrough: write out your actual week 1 onboarding process. what do you look at first, what do you fix first, what do you ignore. this works as people hire processes and showing yours makes them think “this person has a system” which is the opposite of “i’ll figure it out as i go”
4. mistake autopsy: write about something that went wrong with a client and what you learned. this is a pattern interrupt as vulnerability is rare in freelancer content. while everyone posts wins, a loss with a genuine takeaway makes people trust you more than another “got my client 10x results” post
5. video introduction: record yourself (facecam preferred) explaining what you do to someone who has zero context. keep it between 1-5 minutes max.

if you don’t have clients yet:

1. reverse engineering pin: pick a brand or creator in your niche and break down what they’re doing well (or badly). “i reverse engineered how {brand} writes their landing pages. here’s what most people miss.” this only work if you can give non obvious and tactical breakdowns proving you have taste and analytical ability. claude breakdowns might not work here if you don’t have a personal edge
2. “what i’d check first”: write what you’d look at in the first 10 seconds of reviewing someone’s {website/content/ads/emails}. having a framework has always proved competence faster than a portfolio. “the first thing i check on any landing page is whether the headline passes the 3 second test” positions you as someone with a trained eye
3. common mistakes : “mistakes i see every day in {niche} that nobody talks about.” you don’t need client wins for this. you just need to have looked at enough bad work to know what bad looks like.
4. “built it for myself”: make something you’d normally make for a client but do it for a fake brand or your own project. a landing page redesign, a content calendar, a sample email sequence. post the output. nobody will ask if you were paid for it but they see that you can do it
5. hot take : any contradictory takes that you have about your industry in specific. people come back to your profile to disagree and end up reading your bio. engagement on a pinned post is free social proof and the replies become your testimonials (only try this if you can’t go with any of the previous options, lowest impact)

applies to both:

- use a real photo of yourself if you’re not anonymous. a face makes people trust you 10x faster than a random pfp
- pin a post that already has replies like “just DMed you” or “how do i hire you.” those replies are social proof that matters more than the post itself. if you don’t have those yet, reply to your own pin with “if you’re dealing with something similar, DM me”

and honestly figuring out your strongest pin angle is the hard part and writing it is easy once you know the angle

so i (again) made a prompt that walks you through 7 questions, finds your best angle from your answers, and gives you the skeleton to write it yourself

no templates or generic “pin a case study” advice

it rather builds the angle around YOUR situation

### 3. filled up my content feed with portfolio posts

you could have the best bio and the best pinned post in world but doesn’t matter if someone cannot see your work in your content

most of the times a potential client checks atleast your last 5-10 posts before they ever DM you

that scroll is your interview before the call and most freelancers fail it without knowing it happened

why this kills your inbound:

when someone is deciding whether to hire you, they’re not reading every post, it mostly skimming like 2 seconds per post, maybe 10 posts deep

now they’re looking for a pattern that says “this person knows what they’re doing”

and if the pattern they see is randomness, the answer is no and they won’t even know why, because they just “didn’t feel it.”

what “portfolio posts” actually means:

it doesn’t mean you only post about your work. it means that when someone scrolls your last 10 posts, at least 7 of them should make a potential client think one of these things:

- “this person clearly deals with the same problems i have”
- “they’ve seen this before and know how to handle it”
- “they have opinions i haven’t heard anywhere else”
- “i want to know what they’d say about MY situation”

the other 3 can be personality, humor, whatever, that’s what makes you human but the ratio matters.

if more than half your feed is noise, you’re invisible to the people who would’ve paid you.

### 4. just started being more consistent

honestly i might be the best example of it

you book a client, go heads down, stop posting project ends, you come back, nobody remembers you and this repeats forever.

but now how i fix this is hell simple

just batch 2 weeks of content in one sitting before you start any new project**

and you don’t need to be a creative ass to do it, here’s how:

the day you sign a new client, before you start any work, block 2 hours and write 10-14 posts using this formula:

- 3 posts about what you’re about to work on (without naming the client). “starting a new project where the biggest challenge is [X], here’s how i’m thinking about it”
- 3 opinion posts about something in your niche you’ve been thinking about. these don’t expire, post them whenever (can use claude for inspiration but never draft with it)
- 3 “mistakes i keep seeing” posts pulled from your last client’s situation
- 1-2 questions to your audience. “what’s your biggest struggle with [thing you solve]?” these take 30 seconds to write and keep your replies active

schedule all of them or save it something if you want to post spontaneous

spread across 2 weeks and now you’re visible the entire time you’re busy without thinking about content once

what this looks like in practice:

bad: you post 5 times in one week, go silent for 3 weeks, come back with “hey i’m back! been busy with client work”

good: you post everyday automatically while you’re deep in the project. when the project ends you haven’t lost any momentum and your next client might already be in your DMs because you never disappeared

that’s it

and honestly the bar for consistency isn’t even “post every day”, it’s “don’t vanish for weeks at a time”

### 5. I was talking to peers, not to clients

scroll your last 20 posts now and see how many of them would only make sense to someone who does the same job as you?

“top 5 hooks that get engagement.”

“this video editing hack changed my workflow.”

“copywriters need to stop undercharging.”

you know who saves those? other freelancers

and you know who scrolls right past? the founder who would’ve hired you

the difference is crystal clear:

❌ “this video editing hack changed my workflow”

 ✅ “this brand’s reels were getting 200 views. changed the hook structure and the first 3 seconds. now averaging 15k”

❌ “here’s how i organize my content calendar”

 ✅ “this founder was spending 4 hours a week on content and getting zero leads from it. restructured what they were posting and where. now it takes 45 minutes and last month 6 inbound leads came from linkedin alone”

❌ “top 5 hooks that get engagement”

 ✅ “rewrote a client’s landing page headline and conversions went from 0.3% to 1.2%. the old one described the product. the new one described the problem”

all what your clients care about is their “problem disappearing”

so show them the transformation, not the technique

also before you hit post, run it through these 3 questions:

> would my ideal client read this or just another freelancer?

> am i showing process or showing a result someone would pay for?

> could a potential client screenshot this and send it to their team saying “we need this”?

if you answer “no” to any of them, flip the angle from what YOU did to what THEY got

### 6. i didn’t had any any lead magnet to show what i’m good at

you’re asking strangers to pay you thousands of dollars based on a few posts and a bio

and most of the times you don’t even have a portfolio to show (i feel you ghostwriters)

but isn’t that too much to ask for?

why a lead magnet changes the game:

it’s a low-stake entry point so instead of “hire me for $3k” it’s “here, try this for free.”

now two things happen:

first, they’ve consumed something you made and they’ve seen how you think, what you prioritize, how you solve problems. that’s more proof of work than any testimonial or case study on your website.

second, the psychology kicks in. they got something valuable for free and now there’s a subtle sense of “this person gave me something useful and i didn’t pay for it.” that makes them way more likely to engage when you DO pitch something paid later

and third, it’s a distribution tool, a good lead magnet drives its own traffic on your socials

“drop a comment and i’ll send you this” works as a CTA on literally any post and now every piece of content you put out has a purpose beyond just getting likes

here’s how to make one right now:

1. think about the last question a client asked you before they hired you. that question is your lead magnet topic
2. answer it in a one-page google doc, checklist, or template. something they can USE immediately
3. make it specific to your niche. “landing page audit for DTC brands” not “marketing tips for businesses”
4. pin it in your bio or use it as a CTA on your next post. “i made this for a client, figured you could use it too”

the idea, the structure, the title, the distribution - all of it came from one framework

### 7. I never showed up where my clients lived

you don’t need to sell fish in a desert

just spot where your clients spend their time, talking about the exact problems you solve, and need to hit them there

here’s how to actually find them:

- search your pain point: use keyword based search to spot people talking about the problem you solve. save that search, check it daily
- follow your competitors’ followers: those are people who already care about the service you offer. now engage with their posts to get their attention
- stalk the “just raised funding” and “just launched” posts: companies that just got money or just shipped something are about to hire. most of the times they are not posting any hiring but in 2 weeks they’ll need help. be in their replies/dms NOW so when they do need someone, your name is already familiar
- find the podcasts your ICP listens to. : the ones founders in your niche listen to. engage with the host’s posts. that comment section is your goldmine full of your buyers and zero sellers

### 8. I never posted my wins

I already knew i should be posting wins but i never did

feels like bragging right?

you think your results aren’t “impressive enough” compared to people posting 10x revenue screenshots

or because you don’t want clients to feel exposed

or because you tell yourself “i’ll post about it when i land a bigger project”

or because you genuinely forget

that’s why this is low leverage for most freelancers and it’s not that they don’t know, it’s that every instinct tells them to skip it

and every time you skip it, someone with half your skill but twice your visibility gets the inbound you deserved

so here’s how you can still post your wins (besides showing your lambo or rich dad clients ofc)

- a client renewed without you asking (yes this is also a win)
- someone referred you without being asked to
- a client replied “perfect, don’t change anything” first draft (WW)
- you got a DM saying “been following your work”
- a client increased their budget mid-project
- a prospect said “i’ve been meaning to reach out for months”
- you solved something in 20 minutes that their last freelancer couldn’t fix in weeks
- a client asked “can you take on more of our stuff”
- you got pulled into a project through word of mouth
- someone you’ve never met mentioned you in a group chat
- a client asked you to train their internal team on what you do
- you hit a personal delivery milestone (50th project, 100th post, 1 year with a client)
- a client’s audience noticed the quality shift after you started
- someone said “how do you do this so fast”
- you got asked to consult on something outside your original scope
- a client went from skeptical in the first call to sending referrals in month two
- you turned down a project because you’re full (being booked IS a win)
- a cold lead came back 3 months later saying “i’m ready now”

none of these require you to flex revenue numbers or expose client details

all of them make a stranger think “this person is clearly good at what they do”

### 9. I leaked my rates publicly

“i’d manage growth on your socials for 2K$/month” pinned on my profile

seems smart right?

“transparent pricing builds trust”

“people want to know what they’re paying before they reach out”

so you put your rates in your bio, pin a price list, or drop your packages in a tweet

but here’s what happens when you do that:

- you’re killing conversations before they start: someone lands on your profile, sees $2k/mo, thinks “too expensive” and bounces. that same person on a 15 minute call would’ve understood exactly why it’s $2k and said yes but now you removed the conversation where the value gets explained.
- you’re attracting price shoppers: the people who filter by price first are the worst clients you’ll ever work with. they’ll compare you to the $500/mo freelancer and pick the cheaper one every time. the people you actually want don’t shop by price. they shop by trust.
- you’re anchoring yourself down: that $2k you posted is now a ceiling for you. even clients who would’ve happily paid $3k for their specific scope now expect $2k because that’s what you told the internet. you negotiated against yourself before the call even started.
- you’re giving competitors your playbook: your pricing structure, your packages, your positioning. all public. someone might just undercut by 20% and runs ads to your audience.

*and here’s the part nobody talks about: pricing too low publicly loses you clients too.

a funded startup sees “$500/mo content strategy” on your profile and thinks “this person isn’t serious.” and they don’t DM you to negotiate up, they just leave

in their head, someone charging $500 can’t handle their scale and you lost them because your price told them you weren’t good enough

and the same person would’ve paid you $2k on a call because the conversation would’ve shown your depth but the number spoke first (and they always do)

both directions kill you

> too high scares off people who would’ve said yes after a conversation

> too low scares off people who assume cheap means amateur

the only winning move is to never let a number speak before you do.

the fix is learning how to price per person, not per service

i used to charge $500/mo flat for the same work i now charge $2k for same deliverables and same time investment

the difference was i started pricing based on what the work is WORTH to that specific client, not what it costs me to deliver

i turned this entire framework into a system -

- prompt that interviews you about the specific client you’re about to pitch, runs every input, and gives you the exact number to charge with the reasoning behind it

- plus a 20-question pre-call prep sheet for when you’re charging premium,

- a pricing psychology cheatsheet,

- and a client chat advisor that tells you exactly what to say when they push back mid-conversation

its everything i use before, during, and after a pricing call

### 10. I was repurposing content the whole wrong way

repurposing content across platforms is smart and everyone should be doing it (don’t tell me you’re not)

taking one idea and spreading it across X, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube is how you stay visible without burning out creating something new every day

but most people repurpose the TEXT word to word, not the IDEA, literally they copy paste the same post everywhere and wonder why it only works on one platform

every platform has a different language, not literally, but structurally

here’s what actually works on each platform:

X/Twitter:

- short, sharp, opinionated
- personality matters more than polish.
- hot takes, contrarian opinions, one-liner observations get engagement
- threads work for depth but the hook tweet needs to stand alone
- people share things that make them look smart or feel called out

LinkedIn:

- story-driven, longer form
- generic observations are dead on arrival
- open with a personal moment or a specific situation vulnerability and “here’s what i learned the hard way” outperforms advice

Instagram:

- visual first, always
- carousels outperform single images for education content
- reels outperform everything for reach
- the caption supports the visual, not the other way around so if your content doesn’t make sense with the sound off, it won’t work most of the time

YouTube:

- depth wins, the same idea
- the hook is everything (first 30 seconds decide if they stay)
- searchability matters here more than any other platform and title and thumbnail do 80% of the work

also i built a system (again lol) around this that turns 1 idea into 20+ posts across 4 platforms without copy pasting a single line

honestly, it took me mass content burnouts to figure out the structure but now its just a repeatable process
