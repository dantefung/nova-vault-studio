# Sidney Brant（@Byysid）：Tech UGC 从 1 位创作者到 $100K MRR——按阶段把选择题做对顺序的完整 playbook

> Sidney Brant 认为用 UGC 扩张并不难，它只是一串按正确顺序、在正确阶段做出的选择题。第一步是先判断自己所处模式：founder content 与 UGC content 不是一回事，Pre-$10K MRR 是你最亲手的阶段，只该雇 1-2 位创作者；不要因为某个 angle 对一位创作者有效就立刻再招 15 人，那往往是 one-hit-wonder。第二步是招愿意和一个没人听说过的品牌合作的人：别假装已经有成熟 program，早期预算结构用 CPM 结算，直到有真实资金或资本为止。第三步只为转化：Pre-$10K MRR 目标纯粹是 conversion 而非 awareness，直接 walkthrough 式内容最有效。第四步先管住它：solo founder 一个人管 1-2 位创作者没问题，5 位以上就变成一个新岗位，需要在 $10K+ MRR 阶段雇人管 UGC。第五步 $10K MRR 后扩团队：简单产品可以养一支更大的团队跑同一个简单 format；复杂产品团队更小（10-25 max creators），retainer 或 CPM 单价也更高；绝不要换掉正在起效的创作者，作者最好的账号跑了将近一年。第六步保护规模化中的支出：遵守 75/25 规则（75% 已知能转化的 format，25% 试验新角度）；受众变宽后把产品变成故事里的解法而不是视频的主题——这正是 Coca Cola 愿意花大价钱让产品在电影里出现 0.2 秒的原因。第七步招第一层管理者，让他们出题、答疑、每周发一份趋势与团队表现 brief。第八步 $100K MRR 之后：他更看好 in-house，但程序变大后 agency 在法律上更安全，并举最近 Polymarket 的 UGC 项目正因未披露的付费广告与在假平台上摆拍下注的视频而被起诉为例。

## Scaling with UGC isn’t complicated.

It’s literally just a checklist of choices, made in the right order, at the right stage.

Make the wrong choices early, you’ll burn budget on a team that was never going to convert.

But make the right choices, you’ll build a creator engine that compounds itself to the moon.

Here’s my full playbook:

## Step 1: Know which mode you’re in before hiring anyone.

1. 1 - Founder content and UGC content are not the same.

If you’re already a content-driven person, you can run two modes at once. Treat yourself like a creator and test angles yourself before hiring out.

If you’re not, bring in someone who knows what they’re doing and can test a bunch of different angles at volume.

Not being good on camera AND not being good at scripting is the recipe for wasting time on formats that don’t work for you, but could work for someone else.

Founder content is still worth doing regardless.

It’s more personal and builds a story people connect to and you should be doing it even if you lowkey suck on camera.

1. 2 - Pre-$10K MRR is the most hands on you’ll be.

At this stage, it’s safe to assume you’re bootstrapped so the hardest part will be the beginning because you have to figure out what converts before scale.

You should hire 1-2 creators in this stage.

The group should be close knit, you should be able to be direct and nitpicky about what’s on the screen, workshopping which features show up well on camera, and which angles/formats will scale best.

1. 3 - Don’t scale just because one angle worked.

I’ve seen this go wrong so many times.

A content angle performs really well for one creator and the founder assumes they can immediately hire 15 more people because it worked once.

Then they realize it was a one-hit-wonder.

Or it just doesn’t convert anymore because 10 other brands started doing it, saturated the angle, and the audience has seen it too much.

What works for ONE person, doesn’t automatically hold at volume.

## Step 2: Hire creators who’ll work with a brand no one’s heard of yet.

2. 1 - Be transparent.

There are just as many new creators are there are tech founders.

My only rule here is don’t pretend you have a sophisticated program built out already because creators (especially experienced one’s) will know what’s up.

Find someone a bit new.

Tell them about where you’re at and what’s been working for competitors, then let them help you build the model while giving them a reason to stick around before you can pay retainers.

2. 2 - Know your model before posting the opportunity.

The biggest red flag I see is a founder who clearly hasn’t done any research on how this stuff works.

They don’t have a payment structure in mind, asking creators what they want to get paid instead of having the numbers ready ahead of time.

Nobody wants to have that conversation.

Set your general idea and pay scale before you reach out to them.

2. 3 - Early stage budget structure.

Run CPM-based deals until you have real funding or capital behind you.

It’s still a fair deal for newer creators working with a newer team, they’re familiar with it and you’re not spending money on something with no ROI.

Be upfront whether a retainer is the future goal or not.

## Step 3: Optimize for conversion.

3. 1 - Direct, walkthrough-style content wins.

Pre-$10K MRR, your goal should solely be conversion, not awareness.

Unless you’re explicitly trying to sell the company and just need more users to get that deal made.

Show the product, explain it.

3. 2 - Storytime content isn’t worth it yet.

An indirect ad or storytime format might go viral, but it won’t convert at the same rate, and you’ll end up paying for views that don’t turn into users.

Save that format for later.

First get the budget through conversion based content.

## Step 4: Manage it before it manages you.

4. 1 - Solo founders can run 1-2 creators alone.

No one understands your product as well as you, especially if you’re the technical one.

Managing 1-2 people here isn’t the end of the world.

You shouldn’t be drowning in questions, operations, etc yet.

4. 2 - 5+ creators is where it turns into a new role to fill.

Past that number there’s a lot of questions, a lot of responding, a lot of making sure everyone’s on time and invoices are paid.

You have to hire someone to manage UGC at this stage ($10K+ MRR).

Hand off content ideation/management the moment someone on your team proves they understand the product, know the formats, and you’re scaling.

Don’t wait till you’re overwhelmed to let go of control.

## Step 5: Scale the team past $10K MRR.

5. 1 - Simple product, bigger team. Complex product, smaller team.

A simple product with only 1-2 features that’s easy to explain can support a much bigger team running one simple format.

You’ll lose money on some creators, but enough of them will go viral that it balances itself out.

A more complicated product requires a smaller team.

(10-25 max creators)

They’ll do longer videos, that are more conversion based.

You’ll also be paying more per creator through retainer or CPM because you need people who are dialed tf in on content, not just reaction videos.

Just because a creator is good at facial expressions, doesn’t mean they’ll be good at 2-minute walkthroughs - and vice versa.

5. 2 - Never rotate out a creator that’s working.

If they got you to $10K MRR, keep them.

A long-running creator understands your product better than a new hire will and some of my best accounts ran for almost a year because I knew every tiny wording tweak that got more views/conversions.

Also a face never gets saturated to a product.

If anything people will start recognizing the creator and following along even when the content is basically the same ad every day... because the audience likes THEM and relates them to YOUR brand.

## Step 6: Protect your spend at scale.

6. 1 - Budget scales with revenue until the format dies.

Spend more as you make more.

Eventually a format will stop working because the market’s seen it too many times, when that happens, put more effort into testing new formats rather than hiring more creators.

6. 2 - The 75/25 rule.

75% of what you post should be formats you know convert.

25% should go into experimenting and finding new angles.

Make sure your creators always adding something new into the pipeline so you’re never scrambling for ideas the day a format dies.

6. 3 - Content changes as audience widens.

Early on you’re doing product walkthroughs built for conversion.

As you scale past initial users into a bigger, less niche audience, start layering in more storytimes and drama-driven content.

Make the product a solution in the story not the point of the video.

These will convert at a lower rate individually, but it reaches more people and builds bigger brand awareness.

There’s a reason Coca Cola will spend so much to have their product in a movie for 0.2 seconds, it stirs in people’s subconscious.

## Step 7: Hire your first management layer.

Have them manage the team, answer all questions, create and send out a weekly brief on what’s trending and team performances.

This will take that weight off your shoulders.

And it’ll give the team something to reference instead of repeating last week’s content out of laziness (everyone without direction is lazy).

It’s a low effort role but it’ll make a massive difference.

## Step 8: What changes after $100K MRR.

8. 1 - In-house vs Agency.

I believe in-house is the much better alternative.

But legally, agencies are the safer call once your program gets big.

Saw recently that Polymarket’s UGC program is getting sued right now over undisclosed paid ads and staged betting videos on a fake version of their platform.

In-house requires more trust and infrastructure, so until you have that, you might want to choose an agency so nothing crazy happens.

8. 2 - Change your tactics without spend stagnating.

Don’t expect a creator who’s already built a strong account to just keep going more viral than they already have.

Once your team’s in a groove - posting consistently and getting a viral video once a week - the only way to keep scaling organically is to keep adding people to the team.

Past this point, it’s an awareness play to put more eyes on you and less eyes on competitors.

Take your organic winners and start running them as ads.
